# Principles

1. **Make the next story slice possible.** Build enough rules and linked content to explore a meaningful conflict. Expand when the story needs more knowledge.
2. **Keep rules distinct from instances.** A Core rule should judge more than one proposed creation. A named entity, occurrence, sequence, treatment or placement belongs in Content.
3. **Keep one home for each fact.** Link identity, fictional capability, playable treatment and visual representation instead of duplicating them.
4. **Make uncertainty visible.** Record questions, provisional assumptions and intentional mysteries. Do not turn plausible inventions into approved canon.
5. **Review change at its source.** A downstream contradiction returns to its rule or fact owner. Review affected dependents before calling them final.
6. **Protect creative freedom.** Structure clarifies choices; it does not prescribe one act structure, viewpoint, character formula or prose style.
7. **Keep the workflow proportionate.** Derive history and available approval from conversation, documents and Git. Do not add a workflow database, compulsory expert per domain or artistic score.

## Research basis

These principles are a synthesis for this framework, not an industry-mandated taxonomy.

- [Patricia Wrede's worldbuilding questions](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions/) support selective exploration rather than exhaustive prewriting.
- [Emily Short's narrative structures](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/) distinguish branching from other forms of interactive storytelling.
- [Smith and Worch's environmental storytelling](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012696/What-Happened-Here-Environmental) connects fictional events, environmental evidence and player interpretation.
