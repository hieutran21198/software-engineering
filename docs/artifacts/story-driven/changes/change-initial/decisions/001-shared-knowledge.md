# Shared Knowledge with Applicable Medium Rules

## Context

Creators may write a novel and design a game from one world. A game-only model would impose irrelevant mechanics and spatial prerequisites on prose. Separate novel and game world trees would duplicate canon and make revision unreliable.

## Alternatives

1. Keep a game-only framework and adapt prose informally.
2. Maintain complete novel and game knowledge trees independently.
3. Maintain shared Core and Content, with applicable medium rules and linked treatments.

## Decision

Choose shared Core and Content with explicit applicability. Keep prose rules within Narrative Foundation, game rules in their existing domains, and authored treatments separate from fictional identity. Use Markdown and links, with a small iterative workflow and three roles.

## Consequences

Shared facts have one home, while disclosure and player agency can differ by work. The author must distinguish truth, belief, revelation and local outcomes. Optional domains do not block novel work. A domain conflict needs explicit review; the framework does not calculate artistic quality or run games.
