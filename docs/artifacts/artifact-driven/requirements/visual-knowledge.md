# Visual knowledge design

## Need and observed facts

On 18 October 2026 the user asked for evidence-based visual knowledge design principles for ADD and Artifact Plane: research the cognitive science, select representations by the structure of the information, give AI agents operational authoring and validation instructions, apply the principles within the five phases, record the Artifact Plane implications, and show worked examples from this repository's artifacts. The hypothesis to test critically is that complex artifacts become easier to understand when the system selects effective representations of knowledge instead of relying on prose. The change edits the reusable bundle only; application runtime behavior does not change.

Observed facts at revision 126990d (main, 18 October 2026):

- The bundle has 36 Markdown files and no diagram. Its only representation rules are in three places: the Diagram Views section of 02-methodology/dependencies.md (Mermaid `flowchart TD` from identity records, dashed proposed edges, derived task diagrams, diagnostics for invalid plans), the arrow-direction sentence in 06-guidelines/writing-artifacts.md, and the statement in 06-guidelines/system-flows.md that readers derive diagrams from flow records. No page says when a diagram, a table or an example helps, how to keep one consistent with its source, or what a reader shows when it cannot render it. The four role skills and the asd-ste-100 skill govern wording and structure, not the choice of representation.
- The three in-place artifacts hold 116 requirement, specification and decision files. 14 use tables; 25 are decisions, of which 4 compare alternatives in a table; 17 are flow documents whose steps, transitions and supports are YAML front matter; none contains a diagram or an image. A reader of a flow document reconstructs the journey from YAML. For example, knowledge-authoring in artifact-plane has 8 steps, 11 transitions (4 of them backward) and 21 supports, and change-delivery in artifact-driven has 7 steps and 7 transitions.
- Readers differ. The documentation website (Docusaurus with theme-mermaid; mermaid 11.17.2 and the ELK layout are installed) and GitHub render a fenced `mermaid` block as a diagram. Artifact Plane renders Markdown with marked and DOMPurify, forbids img and svg elements, and shows a fence whose language it does not highlight as a plain-text code panel, so a Mermaid block appears as source text. The Plane derives four interactive views from front matter: artifact dependencies, the task plan of a change, the members of a unit and the steps of a flow, with proposed additions and removals marked. It renders no document-embedded diagram and checks no authored diagram against its source.
- Consequence: an author who adds a diagram today has no rule for its text alternative, its source elements, its validation or its maintenance, and the same document renders differently in the three readers.

Research conclusion (the sources, findings and limits are recorded in the specification): the evidence supports selecting a representation by the task and the structure of the information, not adding diagrams everywhere. Diagrams help when readers must find and relate items that prose spreads apart; tables beat graphs for exact comparison; text and visuals help together only when they are integrated, non-redundant and signaled; worked examples help people who are new to a domain, while experts can lose from the same scaffolding; arrows and layout are read as meaning, so a diagram that shows unsupported relationships misleads; language models produce syntactically valid diagrams whose relationships and completeness are often wrong. The hypothesis holds under these conditions and fails outside them.

## Requirements

- V1 Principles. The bundle foundation states the visual knowledge design principles: separate authoritative knowledge from its presentation; reduce unnecessary reading, navigation and mental reconstruction; select the representation from the meaning and structure of the information; connect visual elements to their source elements, dependencies, decisions and evidence; prefer representations that can be regenerated or checked; keep every representation understandable without a renderer. Each principle names its research basis and its limits, and the page distinguishes established findings, design hypotheses and engineering recommendations.
- V2 Categories. The guidance defines three categories with different generation and validation expectations: semantic diagrams, which represent precise, verifiable relationships of the source knowledge; explanatory illustrations, which help communicate a concept without encoding authoritative facts; and interactive knowledge views, which readers derive from structured records for navigation, exploration and editing.
- V3 Selection. A selection guide maps the kinds of information in ADD knowledge (sequence and time, state and lifecycle, hierarchy and containment, prerequisite graphs, behavior graphs with branches and loops, comparison of alternatives, quantities, traceability of evidence, responsibility) to suitable representations and to their prose or table alternative, with the relationships each one communicates, its cognitive limits, its authoring and maintenance cost, its machine-readability, its suitability for generation and validation by an agent, and reader support. It states when no visualization is the right choice. No rule requires a diagram in every document.
- V4 Agent instructions. The guidance gives AI agents a decision procedure for creating, reviewing and changing representations: analyze the structure and purpose of the artifact; identify what prose alone makes hard to understand; decide whether a representation improves comprehension; select it; build the model from authoritative knowledge; generate it; add a concise explanation; keep links to sources and evidence; validate accuracy, completeness and consistency; update or mark affected representations when knowledge changes. It includes self-review criteria and prohibitions: no decorative or redundant visuals, no invented behavior or relationships, no image-generation output treated as verified architecture, no loss of semantic precision, no mandatory diagramming tool, and no replacement of complete knowledge by a simplified picture.
- V5 Phase application. The guidance states how the principles apply within Requirement, Specification including ADRs, Plan, Implementation and Version, and across artifacts (feature evolution, dependency navigation, runtime interaction, change impact, evidence traceability, lifecycle). It adds no phase, changes no ownership, versioning or dependency rule, and keeps methodology phases, runtime states and graph relationships distinct from each other.
- V6 Traceability and maintenance. A semantic diagram names the elements it is derived from by their logical identities and states its derivation rule. Where the facts are structural front matter, the reader-derived view is the primary form, and an embedded copy states that it is derived. Phase self-checks compare representations with their sources; a representation whose source changed is regenerated or marked stale until it is rechecked.
- V7 Portability and accessibility. Representations use a text-defined notation in a fenced Markdown block, with an accessible title and description where the notation supports them, and a text alternative in the document body that carries the same facts. Knowledge documents contain no external images and no executable content. A reader that cannot render the notation shows the source text and the text alternative.
- V8 Worked examples. The bundle shows the principles on this repository's artifacts: one feature followed through at least four phases, with the existing prose, its semantic model, the better representation, the reason it helps, what stays in text and how it stays consistent with its source. Facts are taken from named elements at a named revision, illustrative assumptions are marked, and the example artifacts are not edited for the demonstration.
- V9 Reader implications. The implications for Artifact Plane (rendering, switching between text and view, progressive exploration, navigation from a visual element to its source, agent-assisted generation, visual editing without loss of structure, stale detection, change impact presentation) are recorded as implementation directions, separate from the methodological rules, with no change under apps.
- V10 Consistency and reuse. The canonical bundle and the packaged copy stay identical for every changed file; links resolve and follow the documentation flow; the website builds and renders the new diagrams; Go tests pass. The reusable guidance depends on no path of this repository, so a consumer can apply it in another project.

## Scope
